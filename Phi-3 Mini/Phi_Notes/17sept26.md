Previous devlog recorded 8.347 as a fixed value on three consecutive readings and drew a further inference: that a model whose validation has not moved across 200 steps while its learning rate sits near peak will not turn later under a decaying 
schedule. Phi was placed alongside Qwen as a model without a turn, for the opposite reason, Qwen's being unresolvable and Phi's simply not having occurred. But step 900 falsifies that inference. Validation rose 0.0176, against a range of 0.000693 
across the three readings that preceded it. The departure is twenty-five times the entire width of the plateau it left.

What survives the withdrawal is worth separating from what does not.

The floor is real and the 2x2 stands. Three evaluations within 0.0007 across 200 steps is not undone by a fourth that leaves them; it means the run rested at 8.347 and then began to rise, which is a different and more ordinary shape than an asymptote. 
The comparison against Mistral, vocabulary-matched and differing only in pretraining data, rests on the height of that floor, and the floor is where it was.

The inference about schedules is withdrawn without replacement. A flat validation curve at near-peak learning rate did not predict the absence of a later turn.

### turn vs wobble

The project's divergence criterion requires a monotone training curve alongside rising validation, which is precisely what Qwen cannot supply and why its turn is unresolvable. Phi supplies it. Training reads 8.165028, 8.094781, 8.033964, 7.983980 
across the four evaluations: falling at every interval, with decrements of -0.070, -0.061, -0.050 that are themselves smoothly contracting. Against that, validation moved -0.014, +0.0004, -0.0007, +0.0176. The gap has widened monotonically, +0.182 
to +0.253 to +0.313 to +0.380, with increments of 0.071, 0.060, 0.067. The model is absorbing training text at a steady, slightly decelerating rate and has begun returning less than nothing to held-out data. That is the ordinary signature of a turn, 
and it is the first one this model has offered.

Step 1000 either continues the rise, in which case Phi has a confirmed turn and the lineup's turn morphologies need a new entry for a turn that arrives after a 200-step rest at the floor, or it falls back, in which case 8.364 was a wobble and the 
asymptote reading returns.
### what the loss curve still cannot settle

Unchanged by any of the above. P2's LoRA trains on Finnegans Wake text, so validation can fall on register, punctuation density and common-token distribution with no improvement at Wake-neologism positions. The verdict is the **nv-recall memorisation-versus-transformation test** at `checkpoint-800`: generate, then check outputs against the training text for verbatim spans. Given a P1 that produced base-vocabulary noise without a single Wake token, the prior here is the least favourable in the lineup, which is what makes it the decisive end of the 2x2.
