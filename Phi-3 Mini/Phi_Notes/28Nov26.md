The learning rate fell by a factor of 10.01 between step 2000 and step
2700, and by 30.44 since the best checkpoint. Both curves flattening over
exactly the window in which the step size fell by an order of magnitude is the
expected consequence of the schedule, not a property of the model.

Normalising the validation rise by the integrated learning rate over each
window separates the two:

| Window | val rise | per 100 steps | integrated LR | rise per unit integrated LR |
|--------|----------|---------------|---------------|------------------------------|
| 800 to 2000 | 0.175509 | 0.014626 | 0.01517 | 11.57 |
| 2000 to 2700 | 0.041971 | 0.005996 | 0.00205 | 20.47 |
| 2500 to 2700 | 0.007881 | 0.003940 | 0.00022 | 36.27 |

**Per unit of optimisation actually applied, the divergence did not decelerate.
It accelerated, monotonically, across three nested windows.** The raw
flattening is a schedule artefact, and once the schedule is divided out the
model is overfitting roughly 1.8 times harder in the second window than in the
first, and about three times harder over the last 200 steps than across the
first phase.

The normalisation assumes the loss responds linearly to integrated step size, 
which it does not do exactly, so the magnitudes are indicative rather than measured. 
And the 2500 to 2700 figure rests on two evaluation points spanning a total rise of 
0.007881, which is small enough that the final row should be read as consistent with 
the trend rather than as independent evidence for it. The direction is robust across all
three windows; the factors are not precise.

So: 

> Phi's post-floor divergence shows no sign of saturating. The apparent
> flattening after step 2500 is accounted for by the cosine schedule, which by
> that point had reduced the learning rate to under 10% of peak. Normalised by
> integrated learning rate, the rate of divergence increases monotonically to
> the end of the run.
