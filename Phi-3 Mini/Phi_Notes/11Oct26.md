Four samples per epoch resolves the steps; one sample per two and a half epochs aliases them into a line. The epoch account of post-floor overfitting therefore holds in both models, and the two morphologies are one phenomenon observed 
either side of the Nyquist limit. Phi's per-epoch increment works out near 0.006 at this stage, comparable to Qwen's late risers of +0.012.

A corollary worth recording: 40 steps at an effective batch of 16 implies 640 training blocks, which at `SEQ_LEN 512` is roughly 364,000 tokens, within a thousand of the Llama 8B's 367,022. Two models whose ~44K injected Wake tokens 
cover the same neologisms tokenise the corpus to the same length, which is the expected result and also settles the tokenizer question below.

### continuation 

Unchanged, and it is the whole thing: the nv-recall memorisation-versus-transformation test at `checkpoint-800`. Qwen has set the standard for reporting it, returning no memorisation signal at all across five temperatures, its longest 
span shared with the training text being five high-frequency function words against 219,481 tokens.

Phi is the hard case. Its P1 produced base-vocabulary noise without a single Wake token, the least favourable prior in the set, and it floors 3.64 above Mistral's 4.705 on a configuration matched in vocabulary, Wake share, 
initialisation, family, cadence and hyperparameters, differing only in pretraining data. Whether filtered textbook pretraining permits the Wake register at all, or only the fit, is the question the 2x2 was built to ask and the only 
one left here.
