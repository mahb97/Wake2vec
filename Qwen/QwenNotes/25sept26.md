### the shape

An overnight run to step 2800 resolved the two-plateau reading into something more specific. It is a staircase, and it is regular in three independent ways.

| tread | evals | steps | band | width | mean | above floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 | 1650–2000 | 5.973 to 6.057 | 0.084 | 6.020 | +0.099 |
| 2 | 4 | 2050–2200 | 6.110 to 6.163 | 0.053 | 6.131 | +0.210 |
| 3 | 4 | 2250–2400 | 6.186 to 6.212 | 0.026 | 6.202 | +0.281 |
| 4 | 4 | 2450–2600 | 6.257 to 6.264 | **0.007** | 6.260 | +0.339 |
| 5 | 4 | 2650–2800 | 6.294 to 6.299 | **0.005** | 6.297 | +0.376 |

The treads tighten monotonically: 0.084, 0.053, 0.026, 0.007, 0.005. Five consecutive contractions, ending with four consecutive readings inside five thousandths.

The risers shrink monotonically: +0.111, +0.071, +0.058, +0.037. Ratios of roughly 0.6 to 0.8, which extrapolates to a ceiling near **6.38**, about 0.46 above the floor.

The risers are evenly spaced. They fall at 2050, 2250, 2450 and 2650: every 200 steps, without exception across the four transitions observed. Within a tread, consecutive evaluations differ by 0.001 to 0.007; across a riser, by 0.031 to 0.091. The step changes are one to two orders of magnitude larger than the noise they sit in.

### what the staircase is probably made of

Two mechanisms, each accounting for one of the regularities, and the second is testable.

- The tightening treads are the schedule. The cosine learning rate is annealing, so step-to-step variance in the validation estimate contracts as updates get smaller. That explains why the run has become quieter as it has got worse, and it is also why the four earlier divergence signals were refusable: the band was wide enough at 1650 and 1900 to produce apparent jumps that were noise.

- The evenly spaced risers look like epoch boundaries. Overfitting on a corpus this small does not accrue smoothly; it accrues each time the model completes a pass and sees the same blocks again. A 200-step period would mean one epoch takes 200 optimiser steps, which at GRAD_ACCUM 16 is 3,200 sequences.

- The evenly spaced risers are epoch boundaries, confirmed. The progress bar reads `Epoch 13.88/15` at step 2804, giving 202 optimiser steps per epoch (2804 / 13.88 = 202.0, and ceil(3000 / 202) = 15, matching the displayed total). At GRAD_ACCUM 16 that is 3,232 sequences per pass.

Evaluation runs every 50 steps, so a riser observed at step *n* locates the change in the window (*n* − 50, *n*]. Every one of them contains an epoch boundary:

| riser observed | detection window | epoch boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 2050 | (2000, 2050] | **2020** |
| 2250 | (2200, 2250] | **2222** |
| 2450 | (2400, 2450] | **2424** |
| 2650 | (2600, 2650] | **2626** |

Four transitions, four boundaries, no misses and no unexplained risers. The boundary at 1818 falls inside tread 1, where the noise band of 0.084 is wide enough to hide a riser of this size, and indeed the largest single move within that tread is +0.052 at step 1850, in the (1800, 1850] window.

**So the staircase is an epoch staircase.** Overfitting on a corpus of 3,232 blocks does not accrue smoothly; it accrues each time the model completes a pass and meets the same text again. The annealing schedule supplies the other half: it quiets the within-tread variance enough for the discrete increments to become visible, which is why the structure only emerges after step 2000 and not earlier.
