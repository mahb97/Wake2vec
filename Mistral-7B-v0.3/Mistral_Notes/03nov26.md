**This is not a sample-size effect.** `re-` has 50 pairs, the second-largest group in the set, and sits at z = −0.06, dead on chance. `-ion` has 69 and reaches z = −18.93. The four largest prefix groups (`re-` 50, `be-` 39, `in-` 35, `de-` 33) are all null, while the two largest suffix groups (`-ion` 69, `-ess` 38) split, with `-ion` top of the table and `-ess` marginal at −1.71. `-en`, at 27 pairs, is the most *anti*-consistent group in the inventory: its observed direction vectors agree with each other less than permuted assignment does.

The reading is that Mistral's pre-P3 Wake embedding encodes morphological composition **where the morpheme is a productive, semantically loaded derivational operator with a consistent category-changing function**, and does not encode it where the morpheme is a high-frequency, polysemous, or inflectional one. `-ion` turns a verb into a nominal act every time. `re-` means again, or back, or intensively, or nothing at all.

This is the single most paper-ready result in the file, and it is a *baseline* property: it is what P2 left behind, before P3 touched anything.

### Wake-share control

`morph_wake_share.json` rules out two confounds.

**Every supervised pair is trainable.** 1413 of 1413 pairs, 0 frozen, 1125 distinct Wake rows. The morpheme loss can move every row it supervises, so no part of the convergence above is a masked-gradient artefact.

**Excess does not track Wake density.** Mean `wake_frac_word_tokens` across the 15 significant groups is **0.487**, below the inventory's typical value, and the relation is if anything mildly negative. The extremes make the point directly: `-ious` is 100% Wake tokens and significant (z −3.46), `all-` is 100% Wake and null (z −1.42), `-ation` is 37% Wake and the second-strongest group in the set. The structure is a property of the morpheme, not of how Wake-native its instances are.

### caveat: the final excess mixes two matrices

The `excess vs 0.2843` column throughout the loss table subtracts a null mean computed on `embeddings_pre_p3` from an observed consistency computed on the *current* matrix. For the step-0 row that is exact. For the step-1000 row it is not, because the permutation null is a function of the matrix it is computed over, and the Wake block has drifted (mean row L2 0.027 on norms of 0.40, about 7%).

**The permutation null needs re-running on the final embedding before +0.1346 is quoted as an excess.** The descent in L_morph is unaffected, being a direct measurement, but the excess framing is not yet closed. This is the next analysis to run, and it is cheap.

## Embedding geometry at step 1000

### isotropy: a null, within a narrower scope than the cell claims

| population | pre | post | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| All | 0.891352 | 0.886769 | −0.004583 |
| Base | 0.960480 | 0.960480 | +0.000000 |
| Wake | 0.993415 | 0.993450 | **+0.000035** |

The morpheme loss supervises 1125 distinct rows out of 44,553, which is **2.5% of the Wake block**. The isotropy figure is a partition-function ratio over a 5000-row sample of that block, so roughly 126 supervised rows are in it. A loss that rotates difference vectors within 2.5% of the population, without changing the spread of the point cloud, is not something this instrument can see. 

> Strong lambdas did not move **global** Wake isotropy, measured on a sample in which supervised rows are 2.5% of the draw. They moved L_morpheme 0.6700 to 0.5811 on exactly those rows. The two results are about different populations and do not conflict.

The honest conclusion is that **isotropy is the wrong instrument for the morpheme effect**, and the morpheme excess is the right one. What the isotropy null does establish, cleanly, is that P3 did not disturb the Wake block's overall geometry while reshaping a subset of it, which is a desirable property, not a failure.

`Base` at delta exactly 0.000000 is the strongest confirmation yet that gradient masking held.

The **All** figure dropping −0.004583 while both components are flat is the one genuinely new number here: it means the *relation between* the base block and the Wake block changed more than either block internally. With base-to-new mean cosine at 0.0032 and the norm ratio at 2.37, the mixture statistic is sensitive to small shifts that neither sub-population registers.
